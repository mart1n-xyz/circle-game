# Selected decisions

**Status: defaults selected for the future build.** The user delegated the open choices with “just assume something sensible after an analysis there.” The decisions below implement that instruction. They are game assumptions where the source is incomplete, not amendments attributed to the blueprint's author. The separate instruction to wait for a build request still applies.

The operational specification for these choices is the [v0 scenario](scenario-v0.md), including worked examples and the limits of the analysis. Its numbers are fictional balancing inputs. Parameters can change through later playtesting without reopening every routine choice with the user; change the rules version and update the affected examples.

## Confirmed product direction

- Start as an existing member with allocated funds, monthly contributions, separate personal saving, and other members making the same kinds of decisions.
- Invest personally in outside opportunities, optionally financed by a circle loan; use project proceeds to repay.
- Assess peer applications using imperfect evidence and credibility. Allow strategic disclosure and omissions.
- Adjust contributions and savings, participate in collective decisions, and compare results on a leaderboard.
- Demonstrate the whole designed model through staged delivery. Preserve the supplied blueprint and keep the repository private.
- Prepare documentation now. Implementation starts only when the user asks to build.

## Decisions and reasoning

| ID | Selected default | Reason and source boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D01 | Six seats in one shared circle, 24 monthly decision rounds, then seven run-off rounds. P1 uses bots; P2 admits humans with optional bot seats. Room size is configurable, not a protocol cap. | Members interact over the same funds and outcomes. This gives the requested leaderboard a common context. U03/U09; §14.2 leaves size open. |
| D02 | Equal opening positions; contribution floor 50 credits/month; default contribution 100. Monthly borrower/saver rates 1%/0.1%. Aggregate principal ceiling is eligible owned funds multiplied by 1–2 according to the member's completed repayment record. Exact formula, opening history, and bounds are in the scenario. | Establishes a playable economy and makes contributions useful without buying votes. Own repayment history, not disclosure credibility, changes the ceiling (§§4.4, 6.1, 6.3). |
| D03 | Freeze active voters and application terms. Exclude the borrower and every backer whose pledge covers that application. Approval needs a strict majority of eligible voters and at least two yes votes. Abstention cannot lower the threshold. Competing approved loans use a seeded fair draw after withdrawal settlement. | Selects the source's proposed recusal rule (§§5.2, 18.2). Two independent approvals avoid a sole remaining voter deciding. The draw avoids queue speed or wealth becoming a funding privilege. |
| D04 | Allocate identifiable funds once. Borrower shares, then optional savings, supply own cover; other commitments are excluded. Above own cover require active sureties covering at least 25% of the excess. Revalidate aggregate debt, liens, pledges, and cash at settlement. | The source requires sureties but does not specify full coverage or a percentage. Partial backing keeps the source's reserve/member-loss waterfall meaningful. The percentage and reach into own savings are explicit game interpretations (§§6.1, 8). |
| D05 | One-round notice for optional savings; all due requests share payout capacity pro rata. Settle them before new lending. Keep a 10% member-claim cash buffer for new lending, separately from reserve. Shares leave only through an exit with obligations resolved or set off; outstanding pledges remain locked. | Gives savers a predictable request process without promising instant liquidity. It can still produce a queue: this is a disclosed policy, not deposit insurance (§§4.5, 7.3, 18.4). |
| D06 | One missed due date creates arrears; unpaid arrears at the next due date trigger default. Stop new credit in arrears. Default freezes interest, sets off own funds and pledged cover, then charges reserve and member claims; shares lose before optional savings. Accounting write-off does not erase the remaining recorded obligation. | Project failure and loan default are different events. Selects the source's proposed member-claim priority and fills its timing/discharge gaps. Collections remain limited to paid-in/pledged funds and voluntary payments; no seizure of outside assets (§§6.1, 8). |
| D07 | Retain 25% of positive net surplus. Use the midpoint internal rate, loss carry-forwards in member attribution, bounded direct referrals/founding charges, and proportional scaling if their allocations exceed available surplus. Default discretionary use is a pro-rata dividend on opening shares, amendable by member vote for future periods. | Defines a complete appropriation order without turning growth rewards into debts. Losses are posted once. Already-paid ordinary rewards are not clawed back. P3 rates, caps, advances, and events are in the scenario (§§7, 11, 12, 16.5). |
| D08 | Use finite latent risk bands, a noisy private inspection, optional verified disclosure, unverified claims, and omission. Estimate risk from observable evidence plus an audited disclosure record. Keep repayment history separate. Random later audits compare claims with the original inspection, not with investment success. | Makes ambiguity playable while preserving the distinction between bad luck and dishonesty. A credible borrower can credibly describe a risky project. The entire inference mechanism is a user-requested game addition, not source underwriting. |
| D09 | Three initial project families with upfront costs, 3- or 6-month lives, cash-flow schedules, partial failure recoveries, and shared-sector shocks. All new projects first resolve in a later month. Household income/expenses are explicit external flows; private cash earns zero automatically. | Gives useful borrowing, liquidity management, and risk assessment distinct roles. Circle-funded project purchase is atomic in the simulation; it does not claim the real protocol can police outside spending (§§5.3, 6.1, 7.6). |
| D10 | Rank final net wealth: cash plus recoverable member claims minus unresolved personal obligations. Projects finish before final scoring. Defaulted unpaid balances still count; reserves never do. Publish estimated standings during play and exact settled results only after reconciliation. | Borrowing cannot manufacture points. Choosing continued debt recognition is an explicit game resolution of an unstated source detail, not new collection powers. Compare within the room first. |
| D11 | Bots use ordinary views and commands. Practice advances when ready. Shared rooms default to three-minute planning and two-minute assessment windows; durations are room settings. At timeout, existing authorised payments continue if funded, with no new applications, pledges, or affirmative votes. | A disconnected player should not stop the room or acquire obligations they never authorised. Hosts cannot see hidden state or dispense treasury money. Reconnect and accessibility remain required. |
| D12 | Paid share before admission; two pledged vouchers; event-based release. Use random sampled checks, fixed audit probability, temporary loss of pass-dependent permissions on failure, one named sponsor, and pro-rata residual upstream creditor priority. Full choices appear in the scenario's network section. | These are operational resolutions of §§3, 10–13, 18.4. Checks establish the specified membership eligibility, not project safety. No automatic bailout, extra checker bond, or invented first-unit democracy. |
| D13 | Permit optional charters and community uses of discretionary surplus. Do not invent operating rules for the future bank, housing, healthcare, or insurance institutions. | Preserves the boundary of the designed backbone (§§5.3, 14.3, 18.1). |
| D14 | Use fictional credits and simulated passage, check, and contract events. No real wallets, deposits, identity verification, or blockchain deployment. | A technology abstraction for the browser game, not a financial deployment. |
| D15 | Use a deterministic TypeScript domain engine, browser UI, and authoritative server with transactional storage for shared rooms. Choose current framework/package/hosting details during the authorised build. | These are routine implementation choices, not missing economic rules or another permission gate. No dependencies, application scaffold, or hosting resources are created during planning. |
| D16 | Freeze protocol rules and scenario version for a ranked season. Permit only the documented prospective local votes. Use a secret server seed and stable event-keyed draws. Separate verified multiplayer from local practice. | Makes results replayable and avoids hidden rule changes. This game convention does not resolve the source's undesigned first-unit governance. |

## Interpretation limits that remain visible

Selecting playable defaults does not make the corresponding source gaps disappear. In particular, recusal and shares-before-savings remain labelled **source proposals**; withdrawal terms, risk inference, and the residual-debt scoring policy are **selected game assumptions**.

Section 16.5 mentions losses in both surplus and reserve formulas. The selected accounting interpretation uses one loss posting: current earnings absorb the loss first; any deficit reduces opening reserve and then member claims. Retention applies only to positive net surplus. Do not charge the loss again in reserve.

The source references underlying `design-model.md` and `abstract-model.md` files that were not supplied. Preserve that limitation. If they arrive later, reconcile their rules before claiming fidelity to those separate documents.
